You have built and launched an app with an AI assistant. In this last lesson you look back at the project. A [retrospective](../../../glossary/#capstone-project-retrospective) is a short review of a finished project that records what went well, what went wrong and what you would change next time. You will write one, turn it into a checklist and save it where you will find it again. You finish with a review checklist in your own words.

## Read the history of your project

Open the project on GitHub and go to its commits page. [GitHub's guide to comparing commits](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/how-tos/commit-changes/comparing-commits) explains that the repository's [commit history](../../../glossary/#capstone-project-commit-history) and a single file's history can show different results. A file's history shows only the commits that affected that file, while the repository history shows the broader history of the branch. Read the list from the top to the bottom. Each commit is a checkpoint, and together they tell the story of how the app was built.

Then open the list of pull requests. Each pull request page has tabs for the conversation, the commits, the checks and the files changed, so you can see what you asked for, what the assistant changed and what you accepted.

## Write the retrospective

Write three short lists.

1. **What the AI did well.** Write three things that went faster or better than you expected, such as drafting a page or explaining an error.
2. **What I had to catch.** Write the problems you found by checking, and which check found each one. For example, a change the assistant made that you did not ask for, a wrong answer, or a secret in the wrong place.
3. **What I would do differently.** Write one change to how you wrote the spec, how you asked for changes or how you checked the result.

## Turn it into a checklist

Turn the second and third lists into a review checklist for your next project. Keep it to ten lines or fewer. Each line should be a question you can answer yes or no, for example: "Did I read every change before I accepted it?" and "Does every paid service have a spending limit?" Short checklists get used. Long ones are left in a drawer.

Save the checklist as a file in a repository you will reuse, such as a template you copy into each new project. Commit it with a message, as you learned in Save a checkpoint (commit), so the file stays in the history.

## Write the goal of your next project

Finish by writing one sentence that says what your next project will do and who it is for, using the pattern from Choose your project. Put it at the top of the checklist. Then, the next time you start, you already have a goal, a checklist and a record of what went well.

## Check yourself

- You read the full history of your project, from the first checkpoint to the last.
- Your checklist has ten lines or fewer, and each line can be answered yes or no.
- You can name at least one problem the AI caused that you caught yourself.
- You wrote the goal of your next project.
