Launching means other people can open your app at an address. In this lesson you publish it the way you learned in the publishing course. You finish with a link you can send to a friend.

## What you will do

1. Find out which kind of project you have. If your app is only a website or a browser tool that needs no server of its own and no database, it is a **front-end** project. If it stores data or runs a program on a server, it also has a **back end**.
2. For a front-end project, return to the publishing course and follow it again. Use the option that suits where you live. Let your assistant prepare the publishing settings, and tell it to change nothing else.
3. Push the finished version to the main branch through a pull request, as in the earlier lessons. Wait for the site to update, then open the address in a fresh browser window.
4. Run the done-checklist against the live address, not only on your own computer. Things such as picture paths and page addresses can behave differently when published.
5. Send the link to one person and watch them use it, without helping. Write down where they hesitate.
6. If your project has a back end, be aware that putting a back end on a public server is not covered in this series yet. You may keep it running on your own computer and show it from there. Do not open your computer to the internet unless you know exactly what you are doing.
7. Keep your costs in view for a week: check the services your app uses and confirm the spending limit still holds.

## Check yourself

- A person who is not you can open your app from a link.
- The done-checklist passes on the live address.
- You watched someone use it and wrote down what you saw.
- If there is a back end, you know it is not on a public server yet, and you did not expose your own computer.
