Finishing the project is not the end. In this lesson you show what you built to someone else: what it does, how you made it and what you would change next time. You will write three things: a [README](../../../glossary/#capstone-project-readme) for the project, a three-minute demo and a short project note. Then you will add the project to your [portfolio](../../../glossary/#capstone-project-portfolio), which is your small collection of finished work that other people can look at.

## What the README says

[GitHub's guide to READMEs](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/about-readmes) says that a README tells other people why your project is useful, what they can do with it and how they can use it. It is often the first thing a visitor sees, so write these parts in this order:

1. The name and the one-sentence goal from Choose your project.
2. Who the app is for.
3. A link to the live address from Launch it. The link must not contain a secret.
4. How to run the app on a computer. If the steps differ between Windows and Mac, write them as two short lists, one for each system.
5. What the app does not do yet, copied from your spec.
6. The tools you used, such as the AI assistant you chose in Choose an AI coding assistant. For any service that needs a key, name the setting and never its value, for example `MODEL_API_KEY=YOUR_KEY_HERE`.

## How to give the demo

Plan three minutes in three parts: the problem, which takes about 30 seconds; the main path through the app, which takes about 90 seconds; and what you would build next, which takes about 30 seconds. Show the real app doing its main job, from a fresh browser window. Practise once with a timer, and keep a screenshot ready in case the live link is slow on the day. Recording the demo with a screen recorder is optional.

## What the project note says

The project note is one page with five headings: **Who and why**, **What I built**, **What the AI did**, **What I checked myself** and **What I would change**. In the section on checks, name at least two checks from Check before you ship. This note matters because it shows what you decided, not only what the AI wrote.

## Link it from your portfolio

[GitHub's guide to pinning](https://docs.github.com/en/account-and-profile/how-tos/profile-customization/pinning-items-to-your-profile) says you can pin gists and repositories to your profile so other people can quickly see your best work. Pin the project repository. You may also write a profile README, which [GitHub's guide to profile READMEs](https://docs.github.com/en/account-and-profile/how-tos/profile-customization/managing-your-profile-readme) describes as a README that tells other people about yourself. Keep it short, and link it to the project's README.

## Check yourself

- Someone who only reads your README knows what the project does and how to run it.
- You can give a three-minute demo, and you have timed it.
- Your project note says which checks you did yourself.
- The project is pinned on your profile, or linked from your portfolio page.
