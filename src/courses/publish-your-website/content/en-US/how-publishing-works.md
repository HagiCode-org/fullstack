Your website is a folder of files on your computer. Publishing means putting a copy of those files on a computer that is always on and that anyone can reach. In this lesson you learn the four words that every publishing service uses, so the next lessons make sense. Nothing needs to be installed.

## Words you will meet

**Domain.** The name people type to reach a website, such as `example.com`. A domain is easier to remember than the number that a computer really uses.

**DNS.** The phone book of the internet. It turns a domain into the address of the computer that holds your files. When you connect your own domain, you change an entry in this phone book.

**CDN.** A group of computers around the world that keep copies of your files. A visitor gets the copy that is closest to them, so the page opens faster.

**HTTPS.** The padlock in the browser. It means the connection between the visitor and your site is private. Publishing services usually turn it on for you.

## What you will do

1. Ask your AI assistant to explain each of the four words in one sentence, using your own website as the example.
2. Write down, in your own words, what happens when a visitor types your address: which word comes first, and which comes last.
3. Look at the course list and note the two paths you will meet later: one for readers anywhere and one for readers in mainland China.
4. Decide whether you already own a domain. If you do not, that is fine: every lesson works with the free address that the service gives you.

Never put a password, a key or any private information in your website files. A website is public, and every visitor can read everything you publish.
