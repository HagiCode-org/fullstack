Your website is a folder of files on your computer. Publishing means putting a copy of those files on a server that is always on and that anyone can reach, and giving that server a name people can type. Nothing needs to be installed for this. In this lesson you learn the three words that every publishing service uses, so the next lessons make sense.

## Words you will meet

**Domain.** A [domain name](../../../glossary/#publish-your-website-domain) is a website's address on the internet, such as `example.com`. The name is easier to remember than the numbers that computers use to find each other. Some services give you a free address under their own domain, such as `your-name.github.io`, and you can also buy a domain of your own later.

**DNS.** The [Domain Name System](../../../glossary/#publish-your-website-dns) translates a domain name into the address of the server that holds the site. Every time a browser opens `example.com`, it first asks DNS where that name points. When you connect your own domain, you change an entry in this system so that the name leads to your publishing service.

**CDN.** A [content delivery network](../../../glossary/#publish-your-website-cdn) is a group of servers in many places, each keeping copies of the same files. A visitor is served by the copy that is closest to them, so the page usually opens faster. Some publishing services copy your site to such a network for you, and some do not.

When a site is served over an encrypted connection, the browser shows a padlock. You met that encryption in the previous lesson, under the name HTTPS. Publishing services usually turn it on for you, and the custom domain lesson explains how it is set up for your own name.

## What publishing means in practice

When you publish, three things must be true at once. The files must sit on a server that is running. The domain must point to that server. And the server must answer requests for the paths your pages use, such as `/index.html`. The services in this course take care of the first part for you. You will still check the second and third parts yourself, because they are where most mistakes happen.

Publishing is also public. A published website can be read by anyone who has the address, and search engines and other people can copy what they see. Assume that everything in the published folder is visible to the whole world.

## What you will do

1. Ask your AI assistant to explain domain, DNS and CDN in one sentence each, using your own project as the example. Read each sentence and check that you understand the words it uses.
2. Write down, in your own words, what happens when a visitor types your address. Put the three words in the right order: which one is used first, which one finds the server, and which one serves the nearest copy.
3. Decide whether you already own a domain. If you do not, that is fine. Every lesson in the first part of the course works with the free address that the service gives you.
4. Look through your project folder and list the files that a visitor would receive. Ask yourself whether any of them contains a password, a key or a private note.

**Windows and Mac:** these steps only use your web browser and your project folder, so they are the same on both systems.
