This lesson is a one-page card of the few words you need before you build. You do not need to know how a model works inside. You need to know what you give it, what it costs and where it can fail. You finish with a short list in your own words.

## What you will do

1. Read each word below and write one sentence about it in your own words.

   **Prompt.** The text you send to the model. It holds your instructions and the material to work on. A clearer prompt usually gives a better answer.

   **Token.** A small piece of text, often a short word or part of a word. A model reads and writes in tokens, and services count them.

   **Cost.** Most services charge by the number of tokens you send and receive. A long text costs more than a short one, and a feature used many times costs more than one used once.

   **Limit.** A model can only handle a certain amount of text at once. A text that is too long is cut off or refused. Services also limit how many requests you can send in a short time.

   **Wrong answers.** A model can write an answer that sounds confident and is false. It may invent facts, names or numbers. This is normal behaviour, not a rare accident.

2. Ask your AI assistant: "Explain prompt, token, cost, limit and wrong answers with one everyday example each." Compare its examples with your sentences.
3. Write down your feature from the previous lesson. For each of the five words, note what it means for that feature. For example, for a summary feature, a very long note may hit the limit.
4. Decide one rule for your feature: what happens when the model gives no answer or an obviously bad one. Showing a plain message is better than showing nothing.

## Check yourself

- You can explain all five words without looking at the card.
- You know that a model can be wrong while sounding sure.
- You wrote one thing each word means for your own feature.
- You decided what the app shows when the model fails.
