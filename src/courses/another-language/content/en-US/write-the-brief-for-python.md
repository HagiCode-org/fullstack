A good brief describes the job and not the language. Because you wrote the job down without naming a language, the brief for the Python version is almost the brief you already have. In this lesson you turn it into a request that tells the AI what to build and where it can decide for itself.

By the end you have one page ready to paste to the AI.

## What you will do

1. Start from the job description and the finish checklist you wrote in the first lesson.
2. Add one sentence that names the language: "Build this in Python."
3. Say what the AI may decide. For an app, that may be the Python tools it uses to build the back end. For a script, it may be whether one file is enough.
4. Say what it must not do. For example "do not delete or change my original files" or "do not use accounts or passwords".
5. Say how it must tell you what it did: "Give me the exact command to run it, and a short summary of the files you made."
6. Say how it handles risky actions: "Ask me before anything that deletes, overwrites or sends information out of this computer."
7. Ask the AI to read the brief and list its questions. Answer them in the brief.
8. Save a checkpoint with the brief so that you can compare it with the next versions.

**Safety note.** Do not put real passwords, private keys or personal data in the brief. Test the script on a copy of your files first, not on the originals.

## Check yourself

- The brief says what to build, in what language, and what the AI must not do.
- The brief asks the AI to ask before risky actions.
- Your finish checklist is part of the brief.
