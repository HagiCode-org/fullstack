An assistant that says "done" has only told you what it believes. You are the one who decides whether the work is finished. In this lesson you check the site against a checklist you wrote before you started. By the end you will have a site you have verified yourself.

A done-checklist is a short list of things you can check by looking or clicking. You already wrote one: the "it is done when" part of your brief. Writing it before the work starts keeps you honest, because you cannot adjust the target to fit what you got.

## Good and bad checks

- Good: "The home page links to the about page and the contact page." You can click and see.
- Good: "The page is readable on a phone-sized window." You can resize and see.
- Bad: "It looks professional." Nobody can tick this reliably.

## What you will do

1. Open your brief and copy the "done" part into a checklist.
2. Open the site in your browser and go through the checklist by hand, one line at a time. Tick a line only when you saw it with your own eyes.
3. Note every line that fails.
4. Ask the assistant to review its own work: `Read the brief and the site files. List anything in the brief that the site does not do yet, and anything you changed that I did not ask for.`
5. Compare its answer with your checklist. Where they disagree, trust what you saw, and look closer.
6. Fix the failing lines one at a time, with a commit for each.

## Why the self-review still needs you

An assistant can miss its own mistakes, and it can also report problems that do not exist. Its review is a second opinion that points you at places to look. It does not replace your check.

## Check yourself

- Every line of your checklist is ticked, and you saw each one yourself.
- You read the assistant's self-review and acted on it.
- Your last commit is made after the final check.
