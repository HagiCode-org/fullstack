A brief is a short written request that tells the assistant what to build. The clearer the brief, the less you need to correct later. In this lesson you write one for a small site of two or three pages. By the end you will have a brief you can paste into the assistant, and a reusable template for every later project.

An assistant cannot read your mind. If you write "make me a nice website", it will guess, and its guess will probably not match the picture in your head. Writing five short answers first takes five minutes and saves an hour.

## The five parts of a brief

1. **Goal.** What is this for? One sentence.
2. **Who uses it.** Who will open it, and what do they want?
3. **What it does.** The pages and the main things on each page.
4. **What it must not do.** Things you do not want, such as pop-ups, music or collecting personal data.
5. **How you will know it is done.** Three or four things you can check by looking.

## A fill-in template

```
Goal: <one sentence>
Who uses it: <who, and what they want>
It has these pages: <page name and what is on it>
It must not: <things to avoid>
It is done when: <checks I can do by looking>
```

## What you will do

1. Choose a small site you would like to have, such as a page about a hobby, with a home page, an about page and a contact page.
2. Copy the template into a new file in your `my-first-site` folder and fill in each line.
3. Read the brief out loud. If a sentence could mean two things, rewrite it.
4. Paste the brief into your assistant. Do not press send yet if any line is still a guess.
5. Before building, ask the assistant: `Before you start, list any questions you have about this brief.` Answer them and add the answers to the brief.

## Check yourself

- Your brief has all five parts, and each one is specific.
- The "done" part lists things you can check by looking, not feelings such as "looks good".
- The assistant asked its questions, and your answers are written in the brief.
