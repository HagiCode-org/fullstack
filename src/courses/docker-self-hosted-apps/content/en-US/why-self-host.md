Many useful programs are free to download and run yourself, instead of signing up for a website that runs them for you. Running a program on your own computer or server is called **self-hosting**. In this course you will use Docker, which you already met in the Docker for Beginners course, to run ready-made apps such as a resume builder. By the end of this lesson you will know what self-hosting gives you, what it costs you, and how to decide whether an app is worth running.

## What you will do

1. Read the short list of reasons people self-host: you keep your own data, the app costs nothing to license, you can change how it works, and it keeps running even if a company closes its service.
2. Read the matching list of costs: you must keep the app updated, you must back up its data, and if something breaks you are the one who fixes it.
3. Pick one website service you use today, such as a notes tool or a resume builder. Ask your AI helper: "Is there an open-source app that does the same thing and can run in Docker? Give me three options and say which one is easiest for a beginner."
4. Read the answer critically. Ask the AI which of the three is still actively maintained and where each one keeps its data.
5. Write down your choice and one sentence on why. You will not install anything yet.

**Safety note:** an app that holds your personal information, such as a resume, needs a backup and a strong password. You will set both up in later lessons, so do not put real personal details into an app until you have finished them.

## Check yourself

- You can explain self-hosting in one sentence to a friend.
- You can name two benefits and two costs.
- You have a written shortlist of one to three apps and can say which one you would try first.
