You now have a routine that works for any ready-made app: find its official instructions, have the compose file explained, check the four things, change the secrets, and set up a backup. In this lesson you will use the same routine on two more apps and learn how to judge an app before you install it. At the end you will have tried one simple app and one app with AI features.

## What you will do

1. Start with Memos, a simple note-taking app that runs as a single container. Follow its official instructions, use the routine above, and write a few test notes.
2. Pick one app with AI features, such as n8n (it connects services together and can call AI models) or Open WebUI (a chat page for AI models). Follow its official instructions the same way.
3. If the app asks for a key to an AI service, remember that using that service can cost money. Ask your AI helper to explain how the charges work and how to set a spending limit before you enter the key.
4. Before you choose any new app, check four things. Ask the AI to help you look them up from the project page:
   - Is the project still active? Look at the date of its latest release.
   - What licence does it use, and does that allow your use?
   - Where does it keep its data, and can you export it?
   - Does it need extra programs, such as a database?
5. Add the new apps to your own list with the date, the version and the folder.

**Safety note:** an app that can call outside services can also spend money or send data. Give it only the access it needs, and keep keys out of shared files.

## Check yourself

- Memos runs and keeps a note after a restart.
- You can name the four checks and applied them to each new app.
- You set a spending limit before entering any key that costs money.
