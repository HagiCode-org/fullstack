You have built one site with help and guidance. In this lesson you build a different one on your own, using the same process with a new idea. By the end you will know that you can do the whole job without a worked example in front of you.

## The challenge

Choose one of these, or an idea of your own:

- A page for a club, a team or a small event.
- A menu or price list for a real or imaginary shop.
- A one-page profile of a person you admire.
- A page that explains a hobby to a beginner.

The site should have at least two pages, one section with a list of items, and a layout that works on phones.

## Two pages, one link between them

Each page is its own file. The home page is usually `index.html`, and a second page can be `menu.html`. A [link](../../../glossary/#first-website-link) between them uses the `a` element with an `href` that names the other file. [MDN, a element](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/a) Use the exact file name in the link, with the same spelling and capital letters, or the browser will not find the page.

## Choosing a different tool

If you want the list of items to sit side by side, ask the assistant for a row layout. MDN's guide to flexbox describes it as one-dimensional, which means it lays items out one row or one column at a time. [MDN, Basic concepts of flexbox](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Guides/Flexible_box_layout/Basic_concepts) If you want a different framework from the one the assistant chose last time, ask: `Which tools could you use for this site? Pick the simplest one and explain why.` You do not need to understand the answer in detail. You need to be able to open the result and check it.

## What you will do

1. Create a new empty folder and open it in the assistant.
2. Write a brief with the five parts: goal, who uses it, what it does, what it must not do and how you will know it is done.
3. Ask the assistant for its questions before it starts, and answer them in the brief.
4. Get the first version, open it and write a list of what is wrong.
5. Improve it one change at a time, with a commit after each. Make at least five changes.
6. Check the site at a narrow width.
7. When something breaks, use the routine from **When something breaks** in the course **Build with an AI Partner**: paste the error exactly, say what you expected, and go back to a save point after three failed tries.
8. Go through your done checklist by hand. Then ask the assistant for a self-review, and compare its answer with yours.

## Check yourself

- You built a site with at least two pages without copying the earlier one.
- Every change has its own commit, and you handled at least one problem with the error routine.
- You ticked every line of your checklist by looking at the site yourself.
