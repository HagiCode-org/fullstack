The first version is the assistant's first attempt at your brief. In this lesson you ask for it, open it in a [browser](../../../glossary/#first-website-browser) and record what is wrong. You will not ask for fixes yet. By the end you will have a homepage on your screen and a list of what to improve, with the most important item at the top.

## What you will do

1. Open your `first-website` folder in the assistant.
2. Paste your brief and add: `Build the first version. Keep it simple. Tell me how to open the site in my browser, and list the files you created.`
3. When the assistant finishes, read its summary. If it chose a framework, note its name and the steps it gives for starting the site.
4. Open the site in your browser by following the assistant's steps. MDN's tutorial also asks you to open an `index.html` file inside a web browser, so the idea is the same whichever tool made the site. [MDN, Creating the content](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development/Getting_started/Your_first_website/Creating_the_content)
5. Look at the whole page slowly. Check the name, the introduction, the three projects, the contact line and the colours.
6. Write a list of what is wrong or missing, one item per line, with the most important item first.
7. Compare the site with the "done" part of your brief and mark which checks already pass.
8. Open GitHub Desktop and make your first commit. Write `First version` in the Summary field. GitHub's documentation says that a commit needs a commit message that briefly describes the changes, and the Summary is where you write it. [GitHub Docs, committing changes in GitHub Desktop](https://docs.github.com/en/desktop/making-changes-in-a-branch/committing-and-reviewing-changes-to-your-project-in-github-desktop)

Do not ask for fixes yet. A complete list shows you what matters most, and the next lessons work through it from the top.

## If the site does not open

Copy the exact error text and the steps you followed, then paste them to the assistant. Use the routine from **When something breaks** in the course **Build with an AI Partner**: paste the error exactly, say what you expected, and stop after three failed tries. If the error mentions a setting on your computer that you do not understand, ask the assistant before you change it.

## Why the first version is not the finished site

The first version is a starting point, not a verdict. The assistant made its choices from your brief, and it may have guessed at details you did not write down. Your list is the best guide to what to change next. Keep it in your project folder, so you can tick items off as the lessons go on. When the list is short and the items left are small, the site is ready for the final checks at the end of this course's build work.

## Check yourself

- The site opens in your browser.
- You have a written list of problems, with the most important first.
- A commit named `First version` appears in your GitHub Desktop history.
