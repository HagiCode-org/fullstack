A [brief](../../../glossary/#first-website-brief) is the written request your assistant builds from. A good brief says what the site is for, who will use it, what it must contain, what it must avoid and how you will know it is finished. In this lesson you write the brief for your homepage, using the five-part template from the course **Build with an AI Partner**. By the end you will have a brief that the assistant has already questioned.

## The five parts, filled in for a homepage

1. **Goal.** For example: a one-page personal site that introduces me and shows three projects.
2. **Who uses it.** For example: classmates, friends and people who may want to contact me.
3. **What it does.** A header with my name, an introduction, a projects section with three entries and a contact line.
4. **What it must not do.** No pop-ups, no background music and no forms that collect other people's information.
5. **How I will know it is done.** For example: the page opens from the folder, shows three projects, and every link goes where I expect.

Each part should be specific. "Looks nice" is hard to check. "Three projects in one row on a laptop-sized screen" is easy to check.

## Add a few choices of your own

Describe the feel in plain words, such as calm, bright or playful. Name one or two colours. Say whether you want a light or dark look. You do not need to choose a technology for the site. The assistant may pick a framework, which is a ready-made set of building blocks for making sites. That is fine, as long as the assistant explains what it chose and how to open the site.

It helps to know how a simple site is put together. MDN's tutorial [Your first website](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn_web_development/Getting_started/Your_first_website) starts with the content of a page, written in [HTML](../../../glossary/#first-website-html), and then styles that content with [CSS](../../../glossary/#first-website-css). So your brief can describe what the page says first, and how it should look second.

## Why the done part matters

The last part of the brief is the one you check at the end of the course. Write it as something you can see by looking at the screen, not as a feeling. "The page looks good" is hard to check. "Three projects are visible without scrolling on a laptop-sized screen" is easy to check.

## What you will do

1. Create a file named `brief.md` in your `first-website` folder. Use a plain text editor, which is any editor that saves plain text.
2. Fill in the five parts, using the example above as a guide but with your own details.
3. Add your three project titles, with a one-line description for each.
4. Ask the assistant: `Before you start, list any questions you have about this brief.`
5. Answer the questions and write the answers into `brief.md`.

## Check yourself

- Your brief has five parts, and each one is specific.
- The "done" part can be checked by looking at the site.
- The assistant's questions are answered inside the brief.
